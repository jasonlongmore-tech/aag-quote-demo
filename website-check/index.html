Fixes, most important first

  1. Pick one address and use it everywhere

    High

    Google compares a business's name, address and phone across the web. Right now your listings point to four different places, which makes Google less confident about where you are and can push you down in the Maps results.

    Fix: decide on the address (or "service area only, Denver metro") and update Google, BBB, Yelp, MapQuest and the hidden business info in the website's code to match. The table below lists each one.

  2. Remove the leftover template page

    High

    The page at allaboutglass.co/service-areas is an unfinished template. Its browser title reads "{Business Name} - {Main/broad Business service} in {Service Area}", the headline says "Concrete and Design in Orinda, CA", and the body is placeholder Latin text. It's listed in the site's sitemap, so Google can find it and show it.

    Fix: delete it (the real page is /areas-we-serve) or redirect it there, and remove it from the sitemap.

  3. Merge the duplicate city pages

    Medium

    Boulder and Highlands Ranch each have two pages, for example /service-area/glass-installation-and-repair-boulder-co and /glass-installation-and-repair-boulder-co. Two near-identical pages compete with each other in Google.

    Fix: keep one of each and redirect the other.

  4. Update the review numbers on the site

    Medium

    The homepage says "4.7, based on 64 Google Reviews". Google now shows 4.8 from 103. Showing the current, higher number is free credibility.

    Fix: update the text, or use a live review widget so it never goes stale.

  5. Make the hours match

    Medium

    The site header says Mon–Fri 8 am–5 pm; the Contact page says Mon–Sun 8 am–5 pm. Google's listing should match whichever is true.

  6. Line up the price messaging

    Medium

    The homepage says glass-only replacement "typically runs $200–$500", but the calculator returns $450–$660 for a common 36 × 60 window. A customer who sees both may feel baited.

    Fix: change the headline range to "from $200" or match it to what the calculator actually gives for typical sizes.

  7. Fix the repeated heading

    Quick

    In "6 Signs Your IG Unit Has Failed", the last card is titled "Cold Spots or Drafts" again, but it describes cracked or broken glass.

    Fix: rename it "Cracked or Broken Glass".

  8. Check the owner name on directory sites

    Quick

    MapQuest and Trustindex list the owner as "Darcey Levy". If that's out of date, update or claim those listings.

  9. Website and email use different domains

    Note

    The site is allaboutglass.co; the email is @allaboutglassco.com. That may be on purpose. If not, matching them looks more polished and avoids customers typing the wrong one.

How you show up in search

Searching your company name puts your website first, with your Google panel beside it. People who already know you find you easily.

For general searches like "foggy window glass replacement Denver" and "frameless shower doors Denver", the results were led by Glass Doctor, The Glass Guru, Gatsby Glass and directory sites. Your site didn't appear in the results I saw. That check wasn't run from a Denver location, so local Map results may look better, but these general searches are where new customers come from.

Easy ways to climb those searches

Fix the address mismatch first. Then add real job photos to your Google profile every week or two, list "foggy window glass replacement" and "shower doors" as services there, and ask happy customers to mention the job ("replaced our foggy windows", "new frameless shower") in their reviews. Google reads review text.
